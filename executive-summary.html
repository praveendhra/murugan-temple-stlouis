A Simpler Temple Experience — Executive Summary

A proposed mobile app for the Murugan Temple of St. Louis Prepared for temple leadership · Sources reviewed September 2026

Every app screen in the accompanying deck is a conceptual design. No app exists, nothing has been purchased, and no vendor, payment provider or launch date has been chosen. This document asks for permission to investigate, not permission to build.

The proposal in one paragraph

The temple's website already carries a great deal: the mission, a 2026 calendar, pooja and abhishekam bookings, several routes for giving, a volunteer sign-up and a live-stream page. What it cannot do is reach a devotee between visits. A focused mobile app would bring the schedule, services, sponsorships, giving, volunteering and timely updates into one place a family already carries — and would give volunteers a lighter way to run events. This is a complement to the website, not a replacement for it.

Why mobile adds something a website cannot

What changes
Personal A devotee sees their own RSVPs, sponsorships and receipts, rather than everything at once.
Timely A notification reaches the family who would otherwise miss a time change or a closure. A web page only helps someone who thinks to open it.
Fast Directions, add-to-calendar, watch live and show-my-pass become single taps.
Current Open or closed, today's programme, crowd and parking are accurate at the moment somebody asks.

What we confirmed, and what we did not

Confirmed from the temple's own website: the live page asks devotees to bookmark it and check back, so nothing tells a family a stream has started; attendance, abhishekam sponsorship and donations all move through the same store and cart; the calendar, digital calendar and cultural events sit on separate pages; volunteer sign-up is an embedded form unconnected to any event; and opening hours are not published on the site.

Not confirmed — proposed as hypotheses for discovery: that staff spend meaningful time answering the same questions; that there is no fast way to reach families about a change; and that volunteer coordination outside the event listing creates real workload. These are labelled as hypotheses throughout the deck rather than presented as findings.

What a first version would contain

Home with hours and status · events and family RSVP · reminders and change alerts · a manually updated crowd status · QR and name-based check-in · volunteer sign-up attached to each event · entry points for donation and sponsorship.

Deliberately left for later: automatic crowd counting, Wallet passes and widgets, in-app payments if the temple prefers its existing route, multi-language until confirmed, streaming built into the app, and anything requiring a new vendor.

How the crowd meter would work honestly

Four states — Quiet, Moderate, Busy, At Capacity — each carrying a word and an icon, never colour alone. The screen shows when the status was last updated, gives a wait estimate only when one is reliable, and states plainly that the figure is an estimate entered by staff. In a first version a volunteer taps one of four buttons. Later, RSVP and QR check-in data could inform it. An anonymous entrance counter would only ever be considered after validation. There is no continuous location tracking at any stage.

Four requests, kept clearly apart

  1. RSVP to attend — free, ends with a confirmation and an optional QR pass.
  2. Sponsor a pooja or abhishekam — ends with a sponsorship confirmation and a receipt.
  3. Request a private priest service — a request, not a booking; the temple office confirms.
  4. Donate — one-time, monthly, square foot or stock; ends with a receipt.

These must never share a single checkout.

Protecting devotees who do not use apps

Every action in the app has a route that is not the app: telephone the office, or ask a volunteer at the desk. A volunteer can add an RSVP on a family's behalf and find that family by name without a code. Type is large, contrast is strong, tap targets are big, and status is never conveyed by colour alone.

How this gets tested before any real investment

A short discovery phase, a clickable prototype, then a limited pilot on one recurring pooja and one major festival — the first to see whether a habit forms, the second to see whether the app holds up under crowd, parking and check-in pressure.

The pilot would measure RSVP completion, attendance against RSVP, notification opt-in, reminder engagement, crowd-meter use, check-in success, volunteer participation, donation completion, reduction in repeat inquiries, administrative time saved and devotee satisfaction. No numerical targets are proposed — the pilot establishes the baseline.

Duration and cost are estimated after discovery, not before. Known cost drivers: the number of integrations required, whether payments run through the existing store or a new route, the volume of content to migrate, and the ongoing volunteer time needed to keep the app current.

What is being asked for

Approval for a discovery phase, interviews, validation of temple policies and constraints, a clickable prototype, and a limited pilot. Not a production build, a budget commitment, a vendor or payment selection, or a launch date.

Appendix B — Assumptions requiring temple validation

Nothing below has been decided. Each item is a question for discovery.

Operations

  • Opening hours and any seasonal variation — not published on the website.
  • Site capacity, parking capacity and overflow arrangements.
  • Accessible entrance details.
  • Who is authorised to update crowd status and to send a notice, and what approval a notice needs.

Services and policy

  • Which services accept an RSVP, and which must remain a request.
  • Capacity or booking limits per service and per family.
  • Cancellation, change and no-show rules.
  • Whether a waitlist is wanted at all.
  • How private priest service requests are currently handled and by whom.

Giving

  • Whether giving remains in the current online store or moves.
  • Payment constraints, processing fees and receipting requirements.
  • Which sponsorship categories should appear in a first version.
  • Whether stock donations stay as an instruction page or become an in-app flow.

People and data

  • What devotee data the temple is willing to hold, and for how long.
  • Consent and privacy-notice expectations, and how these relate to the existing privacy policy.
  • Language needs across the community.
  • Existing tools already in use — including the "Vetrivel App / MTS POS" link in the website menu, which did not load when reviewed and may indicate work already under way.

Content and permissions

  • Which photographs and devotional media may be used, and under what permission.
  • Who owns and maintains announcements, the calendar and event content day to day.

Sample data

  • Every name, date, time, party size, reference number, crowd status, wait estimate and notification message shown in the deck is illustrative. The only real figures are the published giving amounts on the donation screen ($301 square foot, $101 monthly Annadhanam), taken from the temple's own online store.
